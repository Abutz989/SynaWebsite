The manufacturing gap

Complex work still depends on scarce skilled hands.

Advanced manufacturers need to increase capacity, yet many precision processes remain too variable for conventional automation and too dependent on experienced technicians.

01 / The problem

The work needs judgment.
The factory needs scale.

Precision finishing and deburring still rely on experienced technicians because the work requires continuous judgment, controlled contact, and adaptation to small differences between parts.

In high-mix, low-volume production, conventional automation is often difficult to justify. Every new variation can require new programming, fixtures, and process engineering. Manual work remains flexible, but capacity and consistency are constrained by fatigue and the availability of skilled people.

01

Too variable for fixed automation

Rigid paths work best when products and conditions repeat. Precision work often does not.

02

Too complex to simplify

Quality depends on how the tool meets the part, not only where it moves.

03

Too important to leave unscaled

When skilled capacity is limited, critical production cannot expand with demand.

Manufacturers face a persistent gap between adaptable manual work and rigid full automation.

02 / Our approach

Keep the judgment.
Move the physical work.

SYNA is developing human-guided robotics that lets a skilled operator direct a robotic tool while the robot carries out the physical process.

The operator responds to part and process variation in real time. The system is intended to add robotic stability, reach, and repeatability—without requiring the entire task to be engineered in advance as a fixed automated sequence.

Human adaptability

The operator remains responsible for process decisions.

Robotic consistency

The system is designed to support stable execution over longer periods.

A more flexible path to automation

Human guidance aims to make changing work easier to accommodate.

How the operator stays connected

A haptic interface links the operator’s motion to the robotic tool and returns contact forces. The experience is designed around guiding and feeling the task—not writing a new robot program for every part.

03 / The opportunity

A recurring need across advanced manufacturing.

The same challenge appears wherever product variety is high, production volumes are limited, and quality depends on skilled contact with the part.

Relevant manufacturing environments

  • Aerospace
  • Defense
  • Medical
Entry

Focused market entry

SYNA is starting with precision finishing and deburring: a concrete application where variation, contact, and operator judgment make the automation gap visible.

Expand

Broader expansion path

After validating the first application, the same human-guided approach could extend to additional contact-rich industrial processes.

These sectors are examples of high-mix, low-volume advanced manufacturing. SYNA is at the concept and technical-development stage and has not yet validated market or performance claims through a commercial pilot.

04 / The vision

Turn skilled execution into a scalable manufacturing capability.

SYNA’s long-term vision is a practical path from human-guided work to intelligent assistance and gradual automation—built from real process knowledge.

  1. Now

    Human-guided execution

    Start with the operator in control and the robot performing the physical task.

  2. Next

    Intelligent assistance

    Use motion, force, visual, and process data to develop guardrails and preserve expert knowledge.

  3. Later

    Gradual automation

    Automate selected parts of validated processes while keeping people responsible where judgment is needed.

The goal is not to remove human expertise. It is to make that expertise more productive, sustainable, and transferable.
